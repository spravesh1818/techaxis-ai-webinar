Intro to GenAI

What is generative AI?

Generative AI is a family of models that produce new artefacts - text, image, audio, video, code, even 3D - by learning from very large amounts of existing data.
- Working definition for this workshop

The shift

  • Old AI: classify, predict, score.
  • Generative AI: create - draft, design, compose, synthesise.
  • Same maths, very different product.

Why now

  • Cheap GPUs + massive web-scale data.
  • Transformer architecture (2017) unlocked long-range context.
  • RLHF + instruction tuning made it usable.
Mental model

Discriminative vs generative

Discriminative AI

  • Answers "is this X?"
  • Spam detector, fraud scorer, X-ray classifier.
  • Output: a label or a score.
  • Optimised for accuracy on a fixed task.

Existed long before ChatGPT.

Generative AI

  • Answers "give me a new X."
  • Writes the email, paints the cover, codes the function, narrates the video.
  • Output: an artefact - usually open-ended.
  • Optimised for plausibility & usefulness.

This is what we are unpacking today.

RULE If the output is a new thing rather than a label, you are talking to a generative model.

How it is built

From translation research to modern LLMs

History in one minute

In 2017, the paper Attention Is All You Need introduced the Transformer architecture for neural machine translation. Instead of processing words strictly one-by-one, attention let models weigh all words in context, making training faster and quality better. That translation breakthrough became the backbone of today's LLMs.

STEP 1 Tokens Your text is split into sub-word units the model can score.
STEP 2 Embeddings Each token becomes a position in a high-dimensional meaning space.
STEP 3 Prediction The model predicts the next token repeatedly; attention determines which earlier tokens matter most.
STEP 4 Training data Scale training on books, code, articles, transcripts, and the web creates broad pattern fluency.

What this gives us

  • Fluent writing across many domains.
  • Pattern transfer: it can imitate styles it has seen.
  • Compression of human knowledge into one interface.
  • Generalisation: translation-era ideas now power chat, coding, image, and video models.

What this does NOT give us

  • Truth - it predicts plausible, not factual.
  • Memory - no recall between sessions unless you provide it.
  • Judgement, ethics, accountability.

Mental model

The new productivity equation

Output = Human judgement × AI leverage
- The shape of knowledge work, 2025+
  • Human judgement is taste, ethics, prioritisation, customer empathy. AI cannot supply these.
  • AI leverage is throughput, draft volume, language coverage, idea variety. AI is unbeatable here.
  • Multiplication, not addition. Either factor at zero gives zero - you need both.
Anchor

What will not change

Always human

  • Taste and aesthetic judgement
  • Ethics, accountability, the apology when it fails
  • Customer empathy and trust
  • Choosing the right problem to solve

Therefore

Lean into the human side. The more AI commoditises drafts, the more taste and judgement compound in value.

"AI will not replace you. A person using AI well, who also has good taste, will." - the new working line.

Facet 1 of 5

Prompting and evaluation

What to learn

  • The 6-pillar framework: Goal → Context → Constraints → Output → Quality → Iterate.
  • Few-shot, chain-of-thought, role priming, structured outputs, prompt chaining.
  • How to evaluate output: rubric scoring, A/B prompts, regression tests.

How to start

  • Run the companion Prompt Engineering Fundamentals deck (it ships with this one).
  • Save your 5 best prompts in a shared doc this week.
  • Score every AI output on accuracy + usefulness for 7 days.

Prompting is not magic. It is specification under uncertainty - and you can practise it.

Facet 3 of 5

Domain mastery

AI without domain is shallow. AI plus a deep domain is unfair leverage.
- The single most under-appreciated career insight of this era

Why it matters

  • The model knows the average. You know the edge cases.
  • You can tell when it is confidently wrong - others cannot.
  • Customers buy expertise from a human, served via AI.

How to invest

  • Pick one domain you can credibly own in 5 years.
  • Read original sources, not summaries. AI does the summary; you read the source.
  • Keep a "things AI got wrong about my field" doc. It is gold.
Facet 4 of 5

Critical thinking and ethics

Pitfalls to spot

  • Hallucinations - confident, fluent, wrong.
  • Bias - models inherit it from training data.
  • Privacy / PII - what you paste may be logged.
  • IP & copyright - generated assets are murky territory.
  • Over-reliance - skill atrophy when you skip the thinking.

Working rules

Never paste secrets. Verify any fact you would put your name on. Be honest about AI involvement when it matters - to clients, to readers, to your team.

NORTH STAR You stay accountable for the output, no matter how it was produced.
Facet 5 of 5

Build with AI - 5 starter projects

1. Personal chatbot

Wrap an LLM API in a tiny UI. Start here - it teaches the loop.

2. Summariser

Turn YouTube / PDFs / meetings into 5-bullet briefs.

3. Classifier

Tag emails, support tickets, or feedback with categories.

4. RAG retriever

Ask questions over your own notes / docs. Vector DB + LLM.

5. Mini agent

A loop that reads, decides, acts - even if it is one step.

6. (Bonus) Workflow

Automate one weekly task end-to-end with n8n / Zapier + LLM.

You learn 10x more by building one of these than by reading ten more articles.
